# **PBN: TLN Core Rules**

### **Draft 9**

## **0. Traits, Rolls & Botches**

### **The trait roster**

This system runs on V20 traits. Nothing else is in play, and material written for other editions must be mapped before use.

**Attributes** (nine, rated 1–5): Strength, Dexterity, Stamina; Charisma, Manipulation, Appearance; Perception, Intelligence, Wits.

**Abilities** (rated 0–5), in three categories — Talents, Skills, Knowledges — drawn from the permitted V20 list. **Arcane** is the scholarly Knowledge of the supernatural. **Eldritch** is a restricted Knowledge; see §5.

**Psyche** (three traits, rated 1–5): Conscience, Self-Control, Grit. These are the load-bearing walls of who the character is — Stability is built from the first two, and Grit is the last thing standing between the character and the dark.

**Willpower**, as both a permanent rating and a current pool. It is a standalone trait, purchased per §7, and is never derived from other traits. **Health**, seven levels, Bruised through Incapacitated — see **§10** for the health track, wound penalties, soak, healing, and all mundane combat. **Nature** and **Demeanour** archetypes, both mandatory at creation — §5's trigger list references Nature.

**Demeanour has one mechanical hook.** Once per cycle, when your character is acting squarely within their Demeanour, you may reroll one failed Social roll. The mask is armour; it works because it is practiced. Nature has no roll of its own — §5 already prices betraying it.

**Grit** is the resistance trait against fear, horror, and supernatural compulsion to flee or to yield — including any splat's mechanic for being tempted toward its own ending. Where an effect of that kind calls for resistance, **Grit stands in for Willpower everywhere Willpower would appear**: roll Grit at difficulty 6 where a Willpower roll would be made, and defend contests on **Grit \+ highest Art**. Mortals defend on Grit alone. Against NPCs, read the NPC's Grit into the §4 formula in place of Willpower, or simply take the tier subtraction as written.

> **Fear is the soft target, by design.** Grit caps at 5 where permanent Willpower reaches 10, so the strongest fear defence in the game is ten dice while the strongest general defence is fifteen. Terror stays effective against elders that domination cannot touch — which is the correct shape for a horror game, and also why the §5 trigger list runs on witnessing things, not being charmed by them.

**Mapping table.** There is no Resolve, Composure, Integrity, Dodge or Occult in this system:

| As written elsewhere | Reads as |
| :---- | :---- |
| Resolve | **Self-Control** |
| Composure | **Self-Control** — except where the material resists *fear* on Composure, which reads as **Grit** |
| Evasion, Dodge | **Athletics** |
| Occult | **Arcane** |

### **Rolling an Art**

An Art roll is **Art rating \+ Resource Trait**, at **difficulty 6**. Ones cancel successes as normal. (Resource Trait: see §2.)

Attributes never contribute to an Art roll. The mundane sheet governs mundane action; Arts govern supernatural action. This is deliberate and should not be house-ruled into V20's Attribute \+ Ability shape, which would make Arts scale twice with the same purchases.

Nor does anything else: **no Stigmata, splat trait, Merit, or similar bonus ever adds dice to an Art roll.** Such bonuses apply to mundane rolls only. Anything that improves an Art does so from the difficulty side — the lever the Appendix already blesses — or it does not exist.

**Wound penalties do apply to Art rolls** (§10). Injury reduces competence; only Stability is exempt from that, and §5 says why.

**Difficulty caps at 10\.** No roll in this system is made at a difficulty above 10, and an effect whose accumulated modifiers would exceed 10 does not become very hard — it becomes impossible, and is not attempted or paid for. This applies to mundane rolls and Art rolls alike. Staff totalling several modifiers should say plainly when the total has passed the line, because the difference between a difficulty 10 attempt and an impossibility is the difference between a scene and a wasted resource. 

**A Willpower *roll* always uses the permanent rating. The current pool is only ever spent, never rolled.** This covers contests, Attrition, Stability, and anything else that says "roll Willpower" without qualifying which. The same holds for every Psyche trait.

### **Contests**

A **contested defence** — permanent Willpower \+ highest Art, or Grit \+ highest Art — is not an Art roll. §0's bar on Stigmata, splat traits, Merits and similar adding dice to Art rolls governs **activation only**. Such traits may add dice to a defence where they say so. Where an Art is contested, both parties roll at difficulty 6 and the acting character's **net successes** are their own successes minus the defender's.

A defending character rolls **permanent Willpower \+ their highest Art rating** — or **Grit \+ highest Art** where the effect is fear, horror, or compulsion to flee or yield. A mortal defends on **permanent Willpower alone**, or Grit alone against the same, per §8. Where the defender is an NPC nobody is rolling for, use the subtraction table in §4 instead.

Where an effect banks its successes at the moment of creation and is tested later — a ward, a seal, a standing curse — the banked figure becomes a **static threshold**. The party testing it rolls their own defence pool at difficulty 6 and must **equal or exceed** the banked number to overcome it. The banked figure is never rerolled, and the absent creator never rolls again. 

### **Helpless targets are not contested**

An Art used against a mortal who is unaware of it, or who has no supernatural means of resistance and is not actively resisting, is **not contested**. The acting player rolls at standard difficulty and reads the effect from their raw net successes. No subtraction applies.

The subtraction table in §4 governs only targets **actively and knowingly resisting** — supernaturals, warded mortals, and mortals who have been alerted and are fighting back.

An individual Art may declare itself contested regardless, where its effect is severe enough to warrant it. **An Art entry that states its effect is "Contested" invokes this clause**: the contest occurs even where the target is helpless or unaware. The word is a design flag, used sparingly — a helpless mortal still rolls their permanent Willpower against a power that reaches into their mind or their fate.

> The counterweight is already written. §5 charges a point of Stability for having "used an Art on a victim who is helpless or unaware," and states that mortals always count as helpless. So the power is free and the price is narrative — the same design the Cathartic Vice uses. This rule does not make supernaturals stronger so much as it makes them culpable.

### **Botches**

A botch is zero successes with at least one 1 showing.

| Botched roll | Consequence |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Art activation** | The Essence is spent, the intended effect does not occur as aimed, and the player rolls **Backfire** (d10, below). |
| **Willpower vs Attrition (§3)** | Loss of control is immediate and total, and lasts **until the next midnight reset**, not merely the end of the scene. No further roll is permitted. |
| **Willpower on a Stability trigger (§5)** | Lose one additional point of current Stability. |
| **Conscience at 0 Stability (§5)** | Lose 1 permanent Stability, and the character acquires a lasting derangement subject to staff approval. |
| **Contested defence** | Treat the opposing character as having scored 3 additional net successes. |

**Backfire** — roll a d10. Every result resolves in-scene without staff, and a HUD can generate all ten automatically; a 10 additionally writes to the storyteller log.

| d10 | Result |
| :---: | :---- |
| **1–3** | **Fizzle.** Nothing further happens — the lost Essence and the failure are the whole event — and that Art suffers **\+1 difficulty for the rest of the scene.** |
| **4–5** | **Inversion.** A harmful effect fires at full strength on the caster — or on the nearest unintended target, where the caster is not a valid one. A **beneficial** effect lands instead on the **most hostile being in the scene**; where no hostile being is present, resolve as a Fizzle. |
| **6–7** | **Bleed.** The caster takes **1 aggravated** as the working vents through their body. The venting is visibly, unmistakably supernatural to everyone present. |
| **8–9** | **Overrun.** The effect fires at uncontrolled scale — area, duration, or number of targets doubled, whichever axis the effect has — and the caster cannot end or recall it. |
| **10** | **Attention.** The working fails, and for one instant the caster is *somewhere else* — a waking nightmare the mind walls off before it can be kept. The character cannot remember what they saw; they can feel that they saw it. Mark **witnessed something that should not exist** (§5). The HUD logs the event. If the player wants to know what was on the other side, they may submit a **storyteller ticket**, and the memory returns as dreams, on staff's schedule. Nothing about this result requires a ruling in the scene. |

> **Attention runs on pull, not push.** The scene resolves entirely at the table — failure, fear, and trigger are all player-administered, and the trigger is one the §5 list already contains. The walled-off memory is a plot hook the *player* chooses to cash in, or never does; the ticket has no expiry and no obligation, and staff read a queue at leisure instead of ruling at 3 AM. Note what the wall means: the sight itself awards **no Eldritch**, because a memory the mind has sealed off is not knowledge, it is damage. The dreams are another matter. A player who submits the ticket is asking to remember, and remembering is how Eldritch is earned — the dream scenes may award it, may mark further triggers, and staff should say so up front. Asking to know what noticed you is a choice, and it is priced like every other choice in this game.  
>   
> **Attention is splat-agnostic.** What is noticed, and what does the noticing, depends upon the nature of the Art — the thing that attends a botched Working is not the thing that attends a botched Blood Art. And note the loop this closes: Eldritch earned through Attention's dreams is the same Eldritch that qualifies a non-Sorcerer to trigger a prepared Working (Sorcerers §7). Curiosity about what noticed you is, eventually, a qualification.

## **1\. What are Arts?**

The splats we permit use drastically different terminology for the same structural thing: a purchasable tree of supernatural abilities. To reduce the arbitration burden on staff and players alike, all such trees are termed **Arts**.

| Splat | Art | Resource Trait |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Dreamborn \[Restricted\] | Oneiric Arts | Glamour |
| Garou | Spirit Arts | Gnosis |
| Hungry Dead \[Restricted\] | Dharmic Arts | Chi |
| Imbued \[Restricted\] | Sacred Arts | Pneuma |
| Lost | Pact Arts | Glamour |
| Metahuman | Psychic Arts | Psi |
| Mundane Humans | N/A | Vitality (see §4) |
| Sorcerers | Occult Arts | Mana |
| Vampires | Blood Arts | Vitae |
| Wraiths \[Restricted\] | Shadow Arts | Pathos |

Any mechanic referencing "an Art" refers to one such tree, rated 1–5. Any mechanic referencing "your Resource Trait" refers to the row above matching your splat.

**Every Art belongs to a splat.** The table above assigns them, permanently and regardless of who ends up holding one. See §8 for what that means in practice.

**One pool per character.** A character has exactly one Resource Trait and may not hold two under any circumstance. Vitality is not a Resource Trait — it fuels nothing and is tracked separately.

## **2\. Resource Traits & the Essence Pool**

Your **Essence Pool** equals **Resource Trait \+ 5**.

Your Resource Trait is a **derived value**: it rises automatically to match your highest-rated Art and is never purchased directly. Capacity for the energy that fuels the Arts grows alongside proficiency with them.

Because every supernatural character begins with an Aligned Art at 2 (§8), **characters begin at Resource 2, for a starting pool of 7**.

Because Resource derives from your best Art, that Art always rolls double its own rating, and every other Art rolls its own rating plus that same figure. Three consequences follow, and all three are load-bearing:

**No acting pool exceeds ten dice.** Art 5 \+ Resource 5\. Defence pools reach higher — permanent Willpower 10 plus Art 5 rolls fifteen — and §4's subtraction figures already account for it.

**Free Arts are carried by Resource.** A specialist with Resource 5 rolls six dice on a 1-dot Art that costs nothing. Write 1-dot Arts as *reliable utility*, not as weak versions of higher tiers.

**Breadth costs accuracy as well as capacity.** A second Art at rating 2 on a Resource 5 character rolls 7, while their capstone rolls 10\. Generalists are worse at everything, not merely less fuelled.

## **3\. Recovery, Replenishment & Attrition**

### **Ambient recovery**

At **midnight server time**, every Essence-using character regains **5 Essence**, and **every character's current Willpower refreshes fully**. This represents your presumed efforts at replenishing yourself in a splat-appropriate manner during downtime between active scenes — the feeding, meditation, ritual work, and harvesting that happens offscreen and needs no roleplay to justify.

Note that Essence pools run from 7 to 10, so a flat 5 restores most of a young character's capacity and only half of a powerful one's. The stronger you become, the more you depend on active Replenishment, and the more feeding drives your story.

**The presumption can fail.** Ambient recovery assumes you had the ordinary opportunity to sustain yourself. When you demonstrably did not, you receive no Essence at midnight. A vampire locked in a cellar with nothing living to drink, a Garou cut off from every spirit and unable to serve their patron, a sorcerer denied the quiet or the clarity of mind to think, a changeling held somewhere joyless — none of them are quietly getting by. Staff call this, and should call it whenever a story has put a character somewhere that makes the presumption a lie.

> This clause is doing real work beyond flavour. Without it, a captive Nightfolk regenerates 5 Essence every night no matter what, which makes any imprisoned supernatural a renewable battery for whoever holds them. With it, captivity starves — and the captor has to keep supplying their prisoner if they want to keep milking them, which is a far more interesting problem than a locked door.  
>   
> **A splat may write a narrow exception.** Where a splat document states that a specific condition sustains its members even when the ordinary presumption fails, that statement governs, and staff do not call a failure against it. Such an exception must name the condition precisely — a preserved body resting in its prepared tomb, not "when it would be thematic" — and must exempt its holder from nothing else: an exempt character can still be drained to 0 by someone else and still enters Attrition when they are. Splat documents should enumerate their exceptions rather than implying them, and a reader who cannot find the exception written down should assume it does not exist. 

### **Replenishment**

A **Replenishment** is a deliberate in-fiction act that restores your Essence Pool by one per two post rounds. It is visible to anyone present, and is always roleplayed rather than assumed. The mechanics are uniform; the act is not. The exception is feeding, which runs at its own rate — see §4.

| Splat | Replenishment |
| :---- | :---- |
| Vampires | Feeding on living blood |
| Garou | Fulfilling a pact with a spirit, or meaningfully acting according to your patron spirit's ideals |
| Sorcerers | Meditation upon the fundamental theorems of reality as you understand them — as likely to be sitting in lotus position as at a chalkboard writing equations |
| Psychics | Deep rest — sleep, trance, or sensory quiet |
| Lost | Harvesting emotion or wonder from a mortal |
| Hungry Dead | Feeding on the flesh or blood of the living. At high tiers can feed on mere living breath. |
| Wraiths | Acting in direct service of their obsession, or deliberately immersing themselves in events and places that resonate with it. A Wraith who **chooses** to succumb to their archetype's Torment counts as performing Replenishment for as long as the episode lasts. Torment entered through failed Attrition, or any other loss of control, replenishes nothing — the Shadow does not pay for what it takes. |

### **Pushing a Roll**

**Nightfolk** may push past safe limits at the cost of physical harm. On any Art roll, you may pay **one Willpower and one level of aggravated damage** either in place of the Essence cost entirely, or to add a single die to your pool, on a strict 1:1 basis — one Willpower and one aggravated per die.

**Where a mechanic multiplies or surcharges an Art's cost, Pushing may substitute only for the *base* cost.** The surcharge is paid in Essence as written. A drained character can Push a standard activation; they cannot Push their way past a penalty someone wrote in Essence on purpose.

> **Note that the two halves of this are not equally attractive, by design.** Aggravated damage is the worst currency on the sheet for most splats, so buying dice one at a time at that price will almost never be worth it. Substituting for the Essence cost outright is the live option: it is how a drained character still does something at the moment it matters, and because the price is flat regardless of tier, it is overwhelmingly a tool for capstone Arts.

### **Attrition**

You cannot activate any Art with a cost while at **0 Essence**. Free (1-dot) Arts continue to function.

While at 0 Essence, you are in **Attrition**. At the end of each scene you remain there, roll **Willpower** at difficulty 6\. On a failure, you lose control entirely for the remainder of the scene. A botch escalates it per §0.

* **Garou** turn feral: Both hyper-aggressive and territorial as their beast/human balance is lost.  
* **Humans (Mundanes)** suffer a classical reality break (Psychosis).  
* **Lost** become a conduit of the Dreaming, the fears of themselves and those around them leaking out as terrifying waking nightmares.  
* **Metahumans** lose control of their abilities, which fire on their own — forcibly spending Willpower and wounding them to lash out at perceived threats.  
* **Sorcerers** turn sociopathic and obsessive, pursuing the secrets of magic without regard to cost or casualty.  
* **Vampires** and the **Hungry Dead** frenzy, attacking anyone within reach to feed on their essence.  
* **Wraiths** succumb to their obsessions and act them out, no matter how inconvenient or dangerous it is.

## **4\. Vitality & Being Fed Upon**

Predators in this setting take from others to survive. What they draw it out of depends on what their victim is.

### **Mortals: Vitality**

**Vitality** measures how much 'blank' essence a living mortal holds in reserve — what can be taken before the damage becomes structural. Without a Nightfolk Aspect it fuels no Arts, and while some magical tools can draw Vitality off, it is primarily a passive buffer; it exists so that feeding does not immediately carve into Health levels.

**Vitality \= Stamina \+ 7**

Because the pool is built on Stamina, a physically frail person is a smaller reservoir — and a predator who does not check will kill them faster. For mortals, Stamina is a survival stat in a way it never was at the tabletop.

Only mortals track Vitality. A mortal who awakens into a Metahuman or Sorcerer gains a Resource Trait and **loses** their Vitality pool in turn; their previously undefined essence gains a specific nature, psi- or mana-aspected, and all the rules that come with it.

> **Joining the Nightfolk makes you a thinner meal.** An average mortal holds 10 Vitality; a new Metahuman or Sorcerer holds 7 Essence. Becoming supernatural shrinks your reservoir by roughly a third, so a fresh awakening can be drained to zero faster than the person they were last week — and unlike a mortal, they lose their Arts and enter Attrition when it happens.

Vitality returns **at midnight server time** at a rate equal to your **unmarked Health boxes** — an uninjured person recovers quickly, a battered one barely at all. A mortal at Incapacitated recovers no Vitality until they have healed.

### **Nightfolk: the Essence Pool**

A Nightfolk drained loses **Essence** directly. There is no second pool to track — the fuel and the meat are the same substance.

### **Common rules**

Feeding transfers at **1 point taken per 1 Essence gained**, up to the predator's own maximum. Points lost this way cause no wound penalties.

**The cost of feeding.** Draining inflicts **1 lethal damage per 2 points taken**, rounded down. Points taken accumulate against the victim across a 24-hour cycle that **resets to 0 at midnight server time**.

The tally belongs to the **victim, not the predator**. Two vampires feeding on the same person in one night share a single running count, so a shared victim is wounded twice as fast as either predator would manage alone.

This damage heals at the ordinary lethal rate for the victim's kind. It applies to any victim, mortal or Nightfolk.

Because recovery is governed by unmarked Health boxes, harming a victim slows their ability to refill. The loop throttles itself: a predator who gorges is destroying their own supply.

### **Taking a feed**

**There is no feeding roll.** Feeding is an act sustained over time, and the longer it is sustained, the harder it pulls. The rate escalates with continuous contact:

| Rounds latched | Rate per round |
| :---- | :---- |
| 1st | 1 point |
| 2nd–3rd | 2 points |
| 4th–6th | 3 points |
| 7th–10th | 4 points |

The pattern continues — each rate holds for as many rounds as its own value. Transfer remains 1:1, so points taken and Essence gained are the same number, up to the predator's maximum. The predator chooses when to stop; the damage tally does not.

**A sip is quick. A gorge is greedy.** One point takes a single round and, alone in a cycle, rounds to no damage at all. A predator still latched in the fifth round has taken eleven points and dealt five lethal. An average mortal is empty before that round ends.

**Breaking contact ends the feed and resets the rate** to the first row. The cycle tally never resets — that belongs to midnight.

**A resisting victim is a mundane problem.** The mundane sheet governs the chase, the grapple, and the pin — resolve those as ordinary actions. Feeding begins only once the victim is held, willing, or unable to interfere. There is no roll to feed faster; there is only the decision to stay latched.

**A victim secured beyond any capacity to resist** — captive, comatose, hours available — may be drained in summary at staff discretion rather than round by round. The tally, the feeding damage, the Tether, and §5's triggers all apply in full. Summary compresses the time, never the cost.

**This table is the rate for feeding, and only feeding.** §3's flat rate governs every Replenishment that drains nobody.

> **The escalation points both ways.** The safe sip got faster — one round, in and out — so a rotation feeder spends almost no scene time on sustenance. The gorge got faster too, but every round latched climbs the damage tally, so speed past the first round is bought entirely from the victim's body. The choice the table forces is the choice the game is about.

### **Willing donors**

**A willing donor is hurt less, not tapped harder.**

Transfer rate is unchanged. Instead, damage from draining a willing donor is inflicted at **1 lethal per 3 points taken**, rounded down, in place of the usual 1 per 2\.

**Willing means freely willing.** The reduced rate does not apply if the donor is acting under any Art-induced compulsion, a Geas, a Tether, or any other supernatural influence on their consent. Where the donor is another player's character, willing means **that player agreed**, per the standard in §5.

> Note that §5's trigger reads "fed on someone who had **no way to refuse**" — a freely willing donor had a way to refuse, so that trigger does not fire. "Fed to serious injury" fires regardless. You can still hurt a volunteer, and it should still cost you.

### **Past empty**

At **0**, whether that zero is Vitality or Essence, further feeding takes **Health levels** one for one as additional lethal damage. A victim at zero is pallid, freezing, and barely lucid — the line where a careless feed becomes a body. Health damage taken past empty must begin to heal before the drained pool starts refilling.

**Draining is not the only way to empty a pool.** Some Arts burn essence away rather than taking it. Such effects transfer nothing to their user, and therefore accrue no tally, no feeding damage, and no Tether — they are not feeding and this section's costs do not apply to them. Where a splat defines such an Art, it defines what happens when the pool is already empty.

**Nor is pool the only thing that can be eaten.** An Art that converts injury or substance directly into its user's Resource Trait, bypassing the victim's pool entirely, is likewise not feeding for the purposes of the tally — but **Tether applies to it in full**, since the dependency the Utkinov Effect describes tracks repetition on a Nightfolk, not the route taken into them.

> **Two consequences of routing Nightfolk feeding through Essence, both good:**  
>   
> **Being fed upon disarms you.** Anyone drained to 0 Essence cannot activate a costed Art and immediately enters **Attrition** (§3). Predation threatens a victim's control, not merely their health.  
>   
> **A spent supernatural is a poor meal.** Essence depletes through ordinary play, so someone who has just fought through a scene has little left to take.  
>   
> **The healing trap.** For splats that pay Essence to heal wounds, being drained and injured together is a spiral — the resource needed to close the wounds is the resource that was stolen. Staff should know that before it happens to someone.

### **Feeding on Nightfolk**

Foreign essence converts at full value. There is no mechanical penalty for a vampire drinking a Garou or a Hungry Dead drawing chi from the Lost.

The deterrent is social, and it is stronger than any dice modifier. The splats in this setting are one interlinked community rather than separate islands, and Nightfolk fight back — they are more capable on average than any mortal, they have allies, and predation on one of them is an offence the community answers collectively. A predator who takes from a mortal has committed a crime nobody may notice. A predator who takes from Nightfolk has made enemies who will.

> Social deterrence only binds characters with something to lose. An exile, an NPC antagonist, or someone who has already burned every bridge is not deterred at all — which means feeding on Nightfolk becomes the signature of the genuinely dangerous rather than a tactic anyone might try.

#### **The Utkinov Effect: Essence Tethering**

As first detailed by the vampire Utkinov, feeding repeatedly on the same Nightfolk does not bind them to you. It binds **you to them.**

**Tether accrues only from Nightfolk victims.** Mortals never generate it, however regular the arrangement — the Utkinov Effect is a property of aspected essence, not of habit. A predator's herd is safe to keep; it is a *diet of peers* that becomes a leash.

Each time you drain the same victim beyond the first within a lunar month, gain one **Tether** point toward them. At **3 Tether**, you are bound:

Essence drawn from anyone else yields **half value**, rounded down; you have acquired a palate. Any roll to act against your Tethered victim's interests, or to refuse them, suffers **\+2 difficulty**. Tether fades by one point per month of complete abstinence from that person.

The victim need never know. They may work it out.

> This is deliberately the inverse of the classical blood bond. Rather than a control mechanic pointing from predator to prey, it is a dependency pointing the other way — the efficient monoculture becomes a leash, and the farmed victim gains genuine leverage over the thing eating them.

### **Against NPCs**

When there is nobody to roll the opposing half, the acting player rolls alone and subtracts the figure for the NPC's tier from their successes. Remember that this applies only to targets actively resisting (§0).

| NPC Tier | Description | Willpower / Art | Subtracts |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | Bystander, victim, ordinary mortal | 3 / — | **1** |
| 2 | Trained mortal — cop, hunter, soldier | 5 / — | **2** |
| 3 | Capable Nightfolk peer | 6 / 3 | **4** |
| 4 | Elder, ancient, major antagonist | 9 / 5 | **6** |

For an NPC who fits no tier, staff derive the subtraction from the formula in the Appendix. Against fear effects, read the NPC's Grit in place of Willpower, or take the tier subtraction as written.

> **Subtractions above 5 are unreachable by design.** Reserve those figures for antagonists meant to require leverage, preparation, or numbers rather than a good roll.  
>   
> **Circumstance is the main lever.** With peer contests sitting at zero expected net, difficulty modifiers and bonus dice from preparation, terrain and surprise decide outcomes. Staff should grant those modifiers freely.  
>   
> Note that a starting character still cannot reliably affect a *resisting* bystander. That is what §0's helpless-target rule is for. Do not solve it by inflating the pool.

## **5\. Stability**

**Stability** replaces Humanity, Paths, Harano thresholds, Clarity, Dharma standing, and every other splat-specific measure of how well a character is holding together. One track, one number, one set of consequences.

It works like Willpower: a **permanent rating** that rarely moves, and a **current pool** that drifts constantly.

Permanent Stability \= Conscience \+ Self-Control − Eldritch.

Most characters sit between 4 and 8\. Current Stability begins at the character's permanent pool max, drops through the ordinary business of being a monster, and is restored through **Catharsis**. Permanent Stability moves only when a character bottoms out — see *Breaking* below.

### **Eldritch**

**Eldritch** is a restricted Knowledge measuring how much you understand of what lies underneath the world — reality as it actually is, rather than as minds are built to survive it. It is not an advantage. Every dot permanently reduces both your maximum and your current Stability.

**Eldritch is not Arcane.** Arcane is the scholarly study of the supernatural as a subject — histories, taxonomies, primary texts, tradition and doctrine, which powers exist and what their rituals are said to accomplish. It is learned the way any subject is learned, and up to the third dot can be freely purchased at character creation or otherwise—Exceeding that threshold means learning a bit too much about how the cosmos works and gaining attention from entities which are bad for your mental health, resulting in a mandatory point of Eldritch. **Learning Arcane 4 requires Storyteller approval and a Storyteller Scene—Mundanes will have to transition to Sorcerer in the process**. You cannot gain Arcane above tier three at character creation.

Eldritch is what you cannot cite, because knowing it required being there.

> **The test.** Could a competent scholar hand you a source that would let a third party check your work? If yes, it is Arcane. If knowing the thing requires having been present when it was true, it is Eldritch — and there is no citation, because everyone who could have written it down was changed by writing it.

**Eldritch is the gate on sorcery, and the only key to its workshop.** No Occult Art functions at all for a character without Eldritch 1 — the theorems do not work for a mind that has not seen what they describe. Using a working exactly as taught requires only the gate. **Altering an existing working, or improvising one that does not exist, requires Eldritch equal to or above the dot rating of the Art involved** — and since Eldritch is awarded only through play, the sorcerers who can rewrite magic are precisely the ones who have paid their minds for the privilege.

One thing Eldritch grants anyone who carries it: a character witnessing a supernatural working may roll **Intelligence \+ Eldritch** to understand what it actually is and does. No Arcane roll substitutes, because no citation exists for the sight of the thing itself.

> Eldritch never adds dice to an Art roll and never will. It opens doors; it does not push through them. Anything that made Eldritch a stat to farm would invert its meaning — it is the ledger of what the character has lost, and the game reads it that way even when the player has stopped.  
> 

* **Sorcerers begin with Eldritch 1\.** No amount of Arcane substitutes.  
* **Nobody else has Eldritch Knowledge, and nobody may take it at character creation.**  
* After creation it is gained **only through play** — reading what should not be read, surviving contact with something ancient, following a sorcerer's work too closely. **Staff award it; players never buy it**.

### **Losing Stability**

Most scenes run without a storyteller present, so Stability cannot depend on one being there. **The obligation for tracking sits with the player.** When your character does something on the list below, make a note of it. A trigger counts **once per scene**, however many times it fired — the list measures what kind of scene this was, not how often it was that kind.

At the end of the scene, roll **permanent Willpower at difficulty 6**. On a failure, lose **1 current Stability per contributing trigger**. On a botch, lose 1 further, per §0.

A success means it has not caught up with you yet. It does not mean it never happened.

**Triggers — mark one if your character, in this scene:**

* Killed, or left someone to die who could have been saved  
* Fed to serious injury, or fed on someone who had no way to refuse  
* Betrayed someone who had reason to trust them  
* Used an Art on a victim who is helpless or unaware; mortals always count as helpless before an Art  
* Acted on a desire that led to serious harm while in Attrition, frenzy, or any loss of control  
* Witnessed or survived something that should not exist  
* Betrayed their stated **Nature** per their character sheet  
* **Catch-all:** *would this character, at the start of play, have recognised themselves doing this?* If not, mark it. Each character is judged against their own standards, not a universal moral scale.

**A splat document may add triggers to this list for its own members**, and may give staff guidance on the pace at which its members should be awarded Eldritch. It may not remove or soften a trigger written here, and staff award remains the only mechanism by which Eldritch moves.

**In a staffed scene, the storyteller may set the deduction directly**, between 1 and 4, where the scene plainly warrants more or less than the count would produce. That is the exception, reserved for events. The roll is the rule everywhere else, because everywhere else there is nobody to exercise the judgment.

### **Cathartic Vices**

During character generation, players select a single **Cathartic Vice** to serve as an unhealthy outlet for venting psychological pressure. Examples include Debauchery, Fighting, Casual Sadism, Theft, Reckless Indulgence, Compulsive Confession, and Cruelty to Those Who Deserve It.

A Vice should be **specific enough to recognise in a scene and narrow enough to be inconvenient.** "Fighting" is a Vice; "coping badly" is not. Staff approve it at creation, and changing it later requires a story reason.

> **Your Cathartic Vice is not a blank check to be a terrible player.** Before invoking your Vice in a scene, confirm that everyone involved is comfortable with where it is going and that you are not crossing a limit they have set. A Vice that requires another player's participation requires that player's agreement — every time, not once. If in doubt, ask out of character before you write it in character.

**Playing out a Cathartic Vice in a scene restores 1 current Stability.** No roll, no approval, no cost in any other currency.

The price is that it happened. Catharsis is a real scene with real witnesses and consequences. You cannot act out a Cathartic Vice offscreen, in summary, or alone in a room where nothing follows from it.

**Limits:**

* **Once per cycle** (midnight server reset).  
* **A Vice that costs nothing does not work.** If the scene generated no risk, no witness, and no consequence, it was not catharsis, it was a description.  
* **A Vice aimed at another player character needs that player's agreement**, in every instance.

>   
> **Recovery is slower than loss, by an order of magnitude.** A single bad scene can cost 4 points; the Vice returns 1 per cycle. Stability is not a resource players can manage tactically — it is a condition they live in.

### **Breaking**

At **0 current Stability**, the character is at the edge. Any further trigger forces a **Conscience roll at difficulty 6**. Failure costs **1 permanent Stability**, and current Stability resets to the new permanent rating. A botch does the same and adds a lasting derangement, per §0.

Permanent Stability is recovered only through long, staff-approved play — a point at most per month, submitted as a scene log, granted or not. Loss is self-administered; restoration requires someone else to agree it happened. The asymmetry is deliberate: falling apart should be available to a player at three in the morning, while putting yourself back together should not. Buying Psyche traits raises the ceiling this recovery can reach, never the current figure — see §7.

Stability lost to Eldritch is never recovered while the knowledge remains, and the knowledge does not leave.

A splat may define a mechanic that stands in for the first Breaking roll of a lunar month. Where it does, it stands in for that roll and does not stack with it.

**Splat-defined movement of current Stability.** A splat document may define costs to and restorations of *current* Stability for its own members: an Art that is paid for in composure, a condition that bleeds it, or a scene that returns it. Such a mechanic is not a §5 trigger and does not roll against the trigger list — it moves the current pool directly, in the amount the splat states.

Three limits, and they are not negotiable. **Permanent Stability is untouched** by any such mechanic, except where the mechanic is the sanctioned stand-in for a Breaking roll above. **Restoration must be priced.** Catharsis returns 1 per cycle and is the baseline; a splat mechanic returning more than that must be rarer, riskier, or bought with something the character cannot easily replace, and the splat document must say plainly what it is buying with. **The asymmetry in this section holds.** Loss stays available to a player at three in the morning; restoration beyond Catharsis is an event, not a routine.

A splat that defines a restoration route should show its arithmetic where the figure is unusual, so that the next person to read it can tell an intended exception from an error.

### **Low Stability**

| Current | State | Passive (always on) | Active |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **8–10** | **Composed** | — | — |
| **6–7** | **Fraying** | \+1 difficulty on all Willpower rolls to resist Attrition, frenzy, or compulsion. Sleep is poor; you are short with people who do not deserve it. | — |
| **4–5** | **Slipping** | As above. You cannot spend Willpower to override an urge more than once per scene. | Once per scene, when the worst available impulse is genuinely tempting, roll **Self-Control** at difficulty 6 or act on it. **You call this on yourself.** |
| **2–3** | **Unmoored** | As above. Your Attrition madness (§3) can trigger at **any** Essence level, not only at zero. | Roll Willpower at the end of any scene involving violence, fear, or temptation. |
| **0–1** | **Breaking** | As above, and every trigger now threatens permanent Stability. | See *Breaking*. |

**Permanent Stability 1** is the floor. A character who reaches it is no longer reliably themselves: staff conversation, and retirement, NPC conversion, or a final scene of the player's choosing.

> **Low Stability never reduces dice pools.** A character coming apart is not less capable — they are less *in control*, which is worse for everyone near them and more interesting to play than a penalty. The mechanics take away judgment, not competence.

### **Damage from Arts**

An Art that inflicts damage deals **1 level on any successful roll, plus 1 further level per net success beyond the first**. The Art states the damage type.

Where an Art unmakes rather than injures — attacking spirits, corpus, or the animate dead with a power whose subject is existence itself — it deals **aggravated**. Otherwise **lethal** against living beings and physical vessels, and **bashing** where the Art's effect is force rather than harm.

**Art damage is not soaked** unless the Art says otherwise. Soak and armour apply only where an Art produces an ordinary physical effect — a conjured blade, a thrown object, a vessel's fists. A power that withers flesh or unmakes a soul is not stopped by a leather jacket or a thickened hide.

**A damaging Art is not contested.** The caster rolls Art \+ Resource Trait at difficulty 6 and reads damage from net successes per this section. The defender does not roll Willpower \+ highest Art against it, and §4's NPC subtraction does not apply. Cover, range, darkness, and surprise are difficulty modifiers per §10.

**Where the effect crosses intervening space as a discrete stroke** — a bolt, a lash, a thrown or conjured thing, a directed wave — the target may declare a **Dodge** (Dexterity \+ Athletics, difficulty 6\) as an action, and each success cancels one of the caster's successes before damage is read. Dodging is an action and comes out of §10's pool-splitting rules.

**Where the effect originates at the target** — flesh that withers, breath that ignites, a shadow that rises from the target's own feet, a soul unmade in place — there is nothing to dodge and no Dodge is permitted.

Every Art that inflicts damage states which of the two it is. Where an entry is silent, staff rule once and note the ruling.

## **7\. Experience**

### **Income**

**Up to 4 XP per month**, awarded as:

* **1 XP** — free, for being active that month  
* **2 XP** — for activity  
* **\+1 XP** — possible bonus; discretionary, for play that materially improves the game for others

### **Unified cost table**

All costs below are roughly **half V20 baseline**, so that a sim earning typically 3 XP/month remains playable, and so that Arts compete with the rest of the sheet instead of dominating it.

| Purchase | Cost |
| :---: | :---: |
| Attribute | current rating × 2 |
| Ability | current rating × 1 |
| New Ability (first dot) | 2 |
| Willpower | current rating × 2 |
| Psyche trait | current rating × 2 |
| Background (first dot) | 2 |
| Background (thereafter) | current rating × 2 |
| Merits | 2 per point |
| Arts | see below |

Willpower is priced as an attribute rather than at V20's flat rate. It is the universal defence in this system, and at V20 pricing it would be both the cheapest purchase available and the most broadly useful — every spare point would go into it.

**Buying Psyche traits raises the ceiling, never the floor.** Permanent Stability is calculated from Conscience, Self-Control and Eldritch at creation, and thereafter tracked as its own figure. Raising Conscience or Self-Control after a Breaking raises the maximum that permanent Stability can be *restored to* — the restoration itself still comes only through §5's staff-approved route, a point at most per month. There is no purchase that puts a broken mind back together; there are only purchases that leave room for the mending.

### **Backgrounds**

**Backgrounds are story-gated in both directions.** They may only be purchased where play has established the circumstance, and they may be **lost** in play when circumstances change — an Ally dies, a Mentor turns, Resources are seized.

**Where a Background is lost to plot rather than to the player's choice, the XP spent on it is credited back.** Otherwise players will refuse plot hooks to protect their sheets, which is the opposite of what a story-gated trait is for.

Priced at rating × 2 rather than flat, a Background to 5 costs 20 XP — the same as an Aligned Art to 5, which is the right comparison. Both are story-gated, both are transformative at high rating, and neither should be cheaper than the other.

### **Merits**

**2 XP per point**, and story-gated after creation exactly as Backgrounds are: something in play must have caused it.

Merits are **not** refunded if lost, since a Merit describes the character rather than their circumstances. Where a Merit genuinely becomes untrue, staff may permit the points be reassigned to another Merit rather than credited.

### **Flaws**

**None.** No Flaw may be purchased, at creation or ever after. Splat documents may **impose** Stigmata-class traits — drawbacks assigned by the fiction, paying nothing, removable by nothing — and that is the only way a drawback reaches a sheet. A weakness you were paid to take is a discount; a weakness the story gave you is a character.

### **Art costs**

The cost tracks below describe an Art's relationship to you **within your own splat**. Every Art belongs to a splat (§1), and the tracks do not reach across that line — see §8.

* **Aligned/Affinity** — granted by your Clan, Path, Tribe, Patron, Dharma or archetype.  
* **Nonaligned** — an Art of your splat that is nobody's designated Aligned Art.  
* **Discordant** — another group's Aligned Art **within your splat**.  
* **Restricted** — an Art of your splat requiring both a teacher and staff permission before it may be learned at all. Never available at creation; advances on the **Nonaligned** track once learned. Splat-internal, with no cross-splat application.

**Aligned & Affinity Arts** — **20 XP to tier 5\.**

## **8\. Acquisition & Access**

### **At character creation**

During character creation, all supernatural characters start with **one Aligned Art at rating 2** and **another Aligned Art at rating 1**. One of these must be their character's designated “Affinity Art”—a mandatory choice tied to their core identity—Such as a Lilum’s Bloodline Art—though it functions identically to any other Aligned Art in mechanical terms. Note that Restricted and Discordant Arts are strictly off-limits at creation.

**Common Paths are Aligned-track for Sorcerers.** Glyphwork and Scrying are the trade's shared inheritance rather than any School's property. They advance on the Aligned track (20 XP to 5\) for every Sorcerer, and one of them may be taken at rating 1 at creation as the second Aligned Art. 

### **Creation standards**

Every splat document builds on these figures. **They are universal. No splat document may deviate from them**, and no event reward may add to the freebie budget — the Art-dot cap below depends on the 21 being a constant.

| Element | Standard |
| :---- | :---- |
| Attributes | 7 / 5 / 3 across Physical, Social, Mental — priority set by the splat |
| Abilities | 13 Talents / 9 Skills / 5 Knowledges |
| Psyche | 7 dots across Conscience, Self-Control, Grit (minimum 1 each) |
| Willpower | 3 |
| Backgrounds | 5 dots |
| **Freebie points** | **21 — a constant, not a default** |

**Freebie costs:** Attribute 5 · Ability 2 · Willpower 2 · Psyche trait 2 · Background 1 · Merit 1 per point · **Art dot 11, maximum one dot purchased this way regardless of points available**.

Discordant and Restricted Arts cannot be bought with freebies under any circumstance, per this section.

> At 11 against the 21-point budget, the price already enforces the one-dot cap; the written maximum is belt-and-braces for the day the constant slips. The dot is deliberately a poor exchange against XP — the same dot costs 4 XP a month into play — so this is an impatience tax, paid only by the player who wants to *open* as the Aligned Art-3 prodigy and is willing to overpay for the privilege. In a persistent sim, that is a legitimate thing to want.

### **Caps**

**Attributes and Abilities cap at 5 for every character**, mortal and supernatural alike. Psyche traits and Willpower likewise cap at 5 and 10 respectively.

§8's promise that mortals outstrip supernaturals on the mundane sheet is enforced by **XP pressure, not by caps**. A supernatural who keeps pace on attributes and abilities is not buying Art dots, and Art dots also raise Resource. Over a year of play the mortal simply has more of the sheet filled in, and the difference compounds.

> **The mortal endgame is a real future problem and is deliberately not solved yet.** A mortal who maxes their concept eventually has nowhere to spend XP, while a supernatural never runs out of Arts. Two levers exist when that day comes: permitting purchasable Ability specialties, or permitting a single trait at 6\. Do not open either until a mortal actually hits the wall — a cap is very hard to close once opened, and we do not yet know which sink the game wants.

### **Tree limit**

No character may hold more than **four Arts**. Without a cap, veteran characters converge on identical omni-competent kits regardless of concept.

### **Gating Discordant Arts**

XP alone never grants a Discordant Art. Access requires an in-fiction route — a teacher willing to break their own traditions, a pact, diablerie, an unwise bargain — established in play and approved by staff. The XP cost applies on top of the story.

Note that 39 XP is roughly thirteen months of gross income, so a Discordant capstone is a long-campaign achievement and should be treated as scenery rather than a build plan.

### The cross-splat bar

**No character may hold an Art belonging to a splat other than their own.** This is a bar on acquisition, not a surcharge. There is no XP price, no story gate, and no staff approval that purchases a foreign splat's Arts, because no track exists to price them on — Discordant covers another *group*, not another *splat*.

Nonaligned means nobody's Aligned Art **within your splat**. An Art that is Nonaligned to its own splat is simply unavailable to everyone else.

Exceptions exist, they are few, and they are **enumerated in the register below**. An exception that is not written into this section does not exist, however good the story. Staff may add to the register; staff may not grant an unregistered exception at the table.

### Register of cross-splat exceptions

**1.The Sorcery–Psi bar.** No character may hold both Occult Arts and Psi Arts. No exception in this register, present or future, may permit it, and no staff ruling at the table may permit it. An exception may be created only by writing a numbered entry into this register that names the circumstance, the cost, and the approving authority. Until such an entry exists, the bar is absolute; a reader who cannot find the entry written down should assume it does not exist. 

**2\. Mortal awakening.** A mortal may purchase Psi Arts **or** Path Arts as Nonaligned, becoming a metahuman or a sorcerer — never both, never switching afterward, subject to staff approval and an in-fiction awakening. Their Vitality pool is consumed and replaced by an Essence pool (§4). This is a change of splat rather than a crossing.

**3\. Liches.** A Lich retains exactly one Occult Art across the transition from Sorcerer to Restless Dead. It functions as their Aligned Art in every respect, begins at rating 2, advances on the Aligned track, and runs on Pathos. They gain no Restless Dead Aligned Art at creation in exchange. Every other Occult Art the character held is lost permanently and may not be rebought at any price. See the Restless Dead splat document.

**4\. Ghouls.** A living character regularly fed vampire Vitae becomes a Ghoul. Ghouling is a **state, not a change of splat**: the character keeps their splat, their Arts, and their single pool — §1's one-pool rule stands, and a Ghoul never holds Vitae as a pool of their own. For a Nightfolk Ghoul, drinking the domitor's Vitae counts as Replenishment. **While Ghouled, no Art may be learned or advanced.** Whether a Ghoul gains access to any Blood Art, and what Vitae does for a *mortal* Ghoul, is deferred to the vampire splat document; until that document rules, the answer is nothing.

*Further entries to be added as each splat document is finalised. Anything requiring a crossing that is not listed here needs a register entry written and approved before it can be played, not a ruling in the moment.*

Thaumaturgy and Necromancy require a ruling: whether each Path is a separate Art, or the discipline is one Art with Paths as flavour. **Recommend: each Path a separate Art**, or these two trees will outpace every other purchase in the game.

### Mundane humans

Mortals have no Essence and no Arts, and their only pool is Vitality (§4). In exchange:

All XP goes to the mundane sheet, where they will outstrip every supernatural character in attributes, abilities and merits.

**Mortal resistance.** Once per scene, a mortal may spend **1 Willpower** to become *actively resisting* against a single use of an Art — forcing a contest that would otherwise have been uncontested under §0 — or, where a contest was already occurring, to reroll a failed defence.

A mortal may awaken as a metahuman or a sorcerer per register entry 2 above.

Mortals defend on permanent Willpower alone — Grit alone against fear — and will lose most contests against Arts. This is correct for the setting, but it should be stated plainly at application so nobody is surprised by their own fragility.

## **9\. Open Items**

1. **Willpower refresh rate.** §3 refreshes current Willpower fully at midnight. If that proves too generous, the lever is a cap on refresh — not a change to the permanent-rating defence pool.

2. **The mortal endgame.** See *Caps* in §8. Two named levers, neither opened.

3. **Splat coverage.** Wraiths is finalised and Sorcerers is in draft. Each remaining splat in §1's table needs a document, and each is likely to expose a class of core error the previous builds could not — the fastest way to find the next problem is to build something new.

   * Finalising the Restless Dead exposed five core gaps, all patched in Draft 9: no called-shot action (§10), no stated difficulty cap (§0), no permission for splat-defined movement of current Stability (§5), no permission for a second health track (§10), and no single definition of the cycle (§3). Four of the five were rules the splat had quietly asserted on core's behalf, which is the failure mode to watch for in every document that follows: a splat stating a universal rule is a core gap wearing a splat's clothes. 

4. **Mortal creation Willpower.** If mortal fragility at creation ever needs a patch, the lever is raising the mortal Willpower baseline above 3 in their splat entry — never a freebie differential, which would invite awakening arbitrage through register entry 2\. Named, unpulled, same philosophy as the mortal endgame.

5. **The paratech arsenal.** §10's weapon table is mundane-only. Paratech and enchanted weapon ratings, armour ratings above 4, and whatever the setting's firearms actually are need a equipment document — or a decision that Artificers and staff price them case by case. The latter is fine until the first firefight over a price.

6. **Death and destruction per splat.** The Restless Dead states its own (corpus, Destruction, Losing an Anchor) and is the worked example every remaining splat should be pointed at: it defines the track, the soak, both healing rates, what happens past Incapacitated for lethal and for aggravated separately, and what happens when the thing anchoring the character is destroyed. Remaining splats each still owe this. It is a checklist item per document, not a rules gap in core.

7. **Willing and unwilling feeding in one cycle.** §4's donor rate is undefined where a victim is drained both ways within a cycle. Three candidate rulings exist — split counters, unwilling rate wins the cycle, or first feed sets the rate. Rule it before it happens; any of the three beats silence.

## **10\. Mundane Action, Health & Combat**

This section exists so that no other book is required to play. It is V20's combat and health system, stated in full, with this system's rulings folded in. Where it is silent, resolve as Attribute \+ Ability at difficulty 6 and move on.

Numbered last to preserve every cross-reference written before it; read it second, after §0.

### **Mundane rolls**

Any mundane action is **Attribute \+ Ability** at **difficulty 6**, ones cancelling, exactly as an Art roll is Art \+ Resource. One success succeeds; more successes do it better. Difficulty moves by 1 or 2 for circumstance, and staff and players alike should move it freely rather than inventing subsystems.

**Splitting a pool.** A character taking more than one action in a round declares all of them first, then splits the pool between them, subtracting 1 die for each action after the first. The largest pool acts first at its own initiative.

**Extended actions** roll repeatedly against a target number of accumulated successes. **Resisted actions** are contested, higher successes winning, exactly as §0 describes for Arts.

### **Initiative**

Roll **1d10 \+ Dexterity \+ Wits**, once per scene unless something changes. Highest acts first, descending. Ties go to the higher Dexterity, then to the higher Wits, then simultaneously.

A round is roughly three seconds, and "round" means the same thing here as it does in §4's feeding table.

### **Attacks**

| Attack | Pool |
| :---- | :---- |
| Unarmed | **Dexterity \+ Brawl** |
| Melee weapon | **Dexterity \+ Melee** |
| Firearm | **Dexterity \+ Firearms** |
| Thrown object | **Dexterity \+ Athletics** |
| Grapple, hold, pin | **Dexterity \+ Brawl** |

All at difficulty 6 unless circumstance says otherwise. **Perception \+ Athletics** covers awareness of an incoming attack where it matters.

**Dodging** is a declared action, rolled as **Dexterity \+ Athletics** (§0's mapping table: there is no Dodge ability in this system). Each success removes one success from the incoming attack. Dodging is an action and comes out of the pool-splitting rules above, so a character who dodges and attacks in the same round does both worse.

**Cover, range, darkness, and surprise** are difficulty modifiers, not subsystems. A rough guide: firing at long range \+1, at extreme range \+2; partial cover \+1, near-total cover \+2; poor light \+1, effective darkness \+2 or impossible. Surprise grants the attacker 2 bonus dice or denies the defender a dodge, at staff or scene consensus.

### **Called shots**

An attacker may declare a specific target on a body — an organ, a limb, a held object, a named weak point — at \+2 difficulty. Success hits what was aimed at; the damage pool is unchanged unless the fiction plainly says otherwise. Called shots exist so that a defended thing can be reached on purpose rather than on a lucky roll, and where a splat document names a vulnerability, this is the action that reaches it. Staff may rule a called shot impossible rather than merely hard — you cannot shoot a heart through a closed vault door — and where a splat states its own difficulty or effect for a called shot against its weak point, that statement governs. 

### **Damage**

**Extra attack successes add damage dice.** The damage pool is the weapon's rating plus any net successes beyond the first, rolled at **difficulty 6**; each success is one Health level of the weapon's type. A damage roll cannot botch — ones simply fail.

| Attack | Damage pool | Type |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Fist, kick | **Strength** | Bashing |
| Club, improvised blunt | **Strength \+ 2** | Bashing |
| Knife | **Strength \+ 1** | Lethal |
| Sword, axe, heavy blade | **Strength \+ 2** | Lethal |
| Light pistol | 4 | Lethal |
| Heavy pistol | 5 | Lethal |
| Submachine gun (burst) | 6 | Lethal |
| Rifle | 8 | Lethal |
| Shotgun | 8 | Lethal |

Paratech and enchanted weapons state their own figures; an Artificer's blade that "deals lethal to Nightfolk" means exactly that, at the base weapon's pool.

**Art damage does not work this way.** §6 governs it — 1 level on any success, plus 1 per net success beyond the first, unsoaked — and the two formulas are deliberately different. The Appendix says why.

### **Soak**

**Soak is Stamina**, plus armour, rolled at **difficulty 6**; each success cancels one level of incoming damage. Soak is never penalised by wounds and is never an action.

* **Bashing and lethal are soakable.** Mortals soak lethal on Stamina like anyone else; the setting has no clause taking that away.  
* **Aggravated is unsoakable.** No Stamina, no armour, no exceptions — except where an Art, Merit, or splat trait explicitly grants aggravated soak, which is a rare and stated thing rather than a default.  
* **Art damage is unsoaked regardless of type** unless the Art produces an ordinary physical effect (§6).

**Armour** adds dice to soak and is stated per item; a rating of 1–2 covers reinforced clothing and light plate, 3–4 heavy or paratech-assisted protection. Heavy armour may carry a Dexterity penalty at staff discretion.

### **The health track**

Seven levels, and **wound penalties apply to every dice pool the character rolls, mundane and Art alike.**

| Level | Penalty | State |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Bruised** | −0 | Bruised and winded; nothing impaired. |
| **Hurt** | −1 | Superficial injury. |
| **Injured** | −1 | Minor wounds. Movement is hampered. |
| **Wounded** | −2 | Serious injury. Cannot run. |
| **Mauled** | −2 | Severe injury. Barely mobile. |
| **Crippled** | −5 | Grievous injury. Cannot act meaningfully without help. |
| **Incapacitated** | — | Unconscious, or conscious and unable to act. |

**Wound penalties do not apply to:** soak rolls, Willpower or Psyche rolls of any kind — including contested defence under §0 and Attrition under §3 — or Stability rolls under §5. Everything else takes the penalty, Art activations included.

> **Why defence is exempt and offence is not.** A badly hurt character who could not resist domination would be trivially taken at exactly the moment the fiction wants them desperate rather than owned. Willpower is the universal defence in this system (§7 prices it accordingly), and a defence that collapses under injury would make every fight a race to wound-then-dominate. Offence carries the penalty because injury genuinely should cost competence — and note that §5 exempts Stability for the opposite reason: coming apart takes judgment, not capability.

**Past Incapacitated**, further lethal or aggravated damage kills a mortal. What it does to each kind of Nightfolk is that splat's business to define, and every splat document must define it.

**Damage from feeding, and pool loss generally, carries no wound penalty** — §4 states this, and it holds here: Vitality and Essence are not Health.

### **Healing**

Mortal healing, from the moment treatment or rest begins:

| Level | Bashing | Lethal & aggravated |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Bruised → Injured | 1 level per hour | 1 level per day |
| Wounded → Mauled | 1 level per hour | 1 level per three days |
| Crippled | 1 level per hour | 1 level per month |
| Incapacitated | 1 level per hour | 1 level per two months |

**Aggravated damage heals at the lethal rate and cannot be accelerated** by any Art, ritual, or splat regeneration unless that effect names aggravated explicitly. This is what makes it the worst currency on the sheet, which is the premise §3's Pushing rule is priced against.

Supernatural healing rates, and what each kind pays for them, belong to the splat documents. Where a splat pays Essence to heal, §4's healing trap applies.

**Health damage taken past an empty pool** must begin to heal before the drained pool starts refilling (§4).

### **Grappling**

Feeding depends on this, so it is stated rather than assumed (§4: "the mundane sheet governs the chase, the grapple, and the pin").

**To grab**, roll Dexterity \+ Brawl against the target's dodge or Dexterity \+ Brawl. Success establishes a hold; net successes above the first may inflict bashing at Strength as a squeeze in the same action.

**A held character** may act only to break free, and does so by rolling Strength \+ Brawl against the holder's Strength \+ Brawl, higher successes winning. Anyone attacking a held character gains 2 bonus dice.

**A pinned character** — held by a grappler who won a second consecutive contest, or who is simply overwhelmingly stronger — cannot act until they break free, and is **held** for §4's purposes. Feeding may begin.

### **States**

**Unconscious, asleep, drugged, or restrained** characters are helpless: they cannot dodge, cannot soak but may still be soaked by armour at staff discretion, and are **helpless before an Art** under §0. Mortals in these states always are.

**Fatigue, suffocation, drowning, falling, fire and exposure** are bashing or lethal at staff judgment, scaled to the fiction — a two-storey fall is a few dice of bashing, a burning building is lethal per round of exposure. Do not build a table; state the stakes in the scene and roll soak.

**Firearms against Nightfolk.** Nothing in this system makes bullets less effective against the supernatural. Where a splat's nature does — and several will — that splat document states it, and states it as an exception to this section.

## **Appendix: Designer's Notes**

Nothing in this appendix is a rule. It is the arithmetic and the reasoning behind the rules, kept for the day someone proposes changing one.

### **The dice math**

At difficulty 6, with ones cancelling, the true expected net yield is **0.4 successes per die** — not 0.5. Every figure below follows from that.

**Graded Art effects should be written in bands of 1 / 2–3 / 4+ net successes.** Because no acting pool exceeds ten dice (§2), anything keyed at 5+ is a tail event against the weakest possible target and does not occur against a peer, no matter how the power is priced.

**The NPC subtraction formula:**

> **Net successes \= your successes − \[(NPC Willpower \+ highest Art) × 2 ÷ 5\]**

The divisor is two-fifths and not one-half, and this matters: halving the defence pool overstates NPC defence by roughly a point across the whole grid — enough that a capstone Art loses to a peer on average, which is not the intended shape of the game. The tier table in §4 is this formula evaluated at four benchmark statlines; staff building a custom NPC apply the formula and round to nearest.

**Expected outcomes against the tiers**, from the ten-dice acting ceiling:

| Art rating (as highest) | Dice | Raw net | vs T1 | vs T2 | vs T3 | vs T4 |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 2 (creation) | 4 | 1.6 | 0.6 | −0.4 | −2.4 | −4.4 |
| 3 | 6 | 2.4 | 1.4 | 0.4 | −1.6 | −3.6 |
| 4 | 8 | 3.2 | 2.2 | 1.2 | −0.8 | −2.8 |
| 5 (capstone) | 10 | 4.0 | 3.0 | 2.0 | **0.0** | −2.0 |

Peer contests sit at zero expected net. That is why §4 tells staff to grant circumstance modifiers freely — they are the deciding factor, by design.

**Art damage** is *1 on success plus 1 per net beyond the first*, and not *damage equal to net successes*, because the latter is the formula splat authors reach for by instinct and it produces powers that deal nothing to a peer. Likewise the no-soak default: if Art damage were soakable, a \+2 soak effect would erase most of a capstone's output, and capstone damage is already thin.

**Why mundane damage rolls a pool and Art damage does not.** Mundane damage is soaked, so it needs the headroom a rolled pool provides — a Strength 3 punch against Stamina 3 has to be able to land *something*, and only variance on both sides makes that work. Art damage is unsoaked, so the same generosity would be unopposed: a rolled pool plus no soak is how a capstone kills a peer outright, which §6 avoids by paying out in flat levels instead. The two formulas look inconsistent on the page and are load-bearing precisely because they are.

**On wound penalties applying to Arts.** A −2 at Wounded costs roughly 0.8 expected successes, near enough a full Art dot, and at Crippled a capstone rolls five dice — worse than a starting character. That is intended: hurting a caster is meant to be a live tactic, and this is the one lever in the game that reaches specialists whose defence pools are otherwise unassailable. It does interact roughly with self-damaging Arts and with splats that pay Essence to heal, and staff should hold the circumstance-modifier lever ready for a wounded character who has earned a break. If capstones prove to collapse in practice, the fix is halving the penalty column for Art rolls only — do not touch the ceiling, and do not exempt Arts wholesale, which would make them the only pool in the game injury cannot reach.

**The §6 cost curve creates an equilibrium worth preserving:** the accuracy gain from tier 1 to tier 5 is only \+4 dice, while the cost climbs from free to 5\. Players will use the cheapest Art that produces the needed effect and treat capstones as emergency measures. This is a feature — but write capstone Arts to be *decisive* rather than merely stronger, or they will never be used at all.

### **The feeding economy**

**The safe sip, worked through.** A Stamina 3 mortal holds 10 Vitality across 7 Health boxes. One point costs a single round, does no damage alone in a cycle, and refills by morning. Four points — two rounds latched, into the third — costs 2 lethal. Draining all 10 keeps the predator latched into the fifth round, costs the victim 5 lethal, leaves 2 unmarked boxes, and drops their recovery to 2 a day while wounds that heal over weeks knit shut. Restraint is not a moral preference here, it is the only sustainable strategy.

**Why there is no feeding roll.** A roll would need a pool, a difficulty, and a botch, and every candidate pool made feeding scale with something it shouldn't — a high-Art predator is not better at swallowing. The escalation table does the work a roll would have done: speed past the first round is bought entirely from the victim's body, and a predator who wants ten points from one target is committing five rounds to a crime in progress.

**Why willing donors yield flat and bleed less.** Making willing donors more *efficient* rewards acquiring willing donors, and the cheapest way to acquire one is to manufacture the willingness — every splat in this game has a power that could do it. Reducing damage instead rewards treating a donor well, which compulsion cannot fake. It also compounds correctly: because Vitality recovery is governed by unmarked Health boxes, a gentler draw leaves a donor who refills faster, so a predator with a genuine arrangement has a better long-term supply than one taking the same points by force.

### **Stability**

**Why the loss is one lever.** An earlier draft made more triggers both raise the roll's difficulty and widen a discretionary deduction — the same dial turned twice, with the second turn left to the player judging themselves at three in the morning. Now the trigger count does one thing: it is the size of the loss if the roll fails. The inputs are identical, the output is arithmetic.

**Why catharsis is free and the price is narrative.** A mechanical cost would turn catharsis into arithmetic and players would optimise around it. A narrative cost cannot be optimised, only paid. That is a plot generator rather than a tax, and it means the most stable characters in the game are the ones with the most complicated lives.

### **Levers deliberately not pulled**

**Resource above its derived value.** Because Resource derives from your best Art, breadth is never rewarded with capacity — three 5-dot trees give the same pool as one. This is intentional; it keeps specialists sharp. But it means there is no "buy more fuel" purchase in the game. If we later want one, the cleanest lever is allowing Resource to be bought *above* its derived value at attribute pricing.

**The mortal endgame.** See §8's caps note and §9. Two levers named, neither opened, until a mortal actually hits the wall.
