0. Scope, and the Two Alignment Ledgers
This is the Mortal entry in the Human splat family. Sorcerer/Gifted, Projector, Ghoul, Kinfolk and Dhampir are their own documents; a character who becomes any of them stops using this one, and Core Rules Section 7 below covers the two routes out.

It is written to require no changes to core. Every mechanic is either a straight application of a core rule or an exercise of a permission core already grants splat documents.

★ The single biggest consequence. The old draft's entire dramatic engine was a human finding out that monsters are real. In 200 TE that is a shrug — a registered Lilum runs a bar three doors down and pays tax. What a human does not know is what is underneath: what happened during the Interstice, what the quintessence reactors actually run on, who or what the Governor is, what the Mistcrown rearranges itself around, and where the Awakened went. 1. Being Human in 200 TE
Humanity may not be the oldest of the world's peoples, but it is the most stubbornly persistent. It survived the Crash, the War of Ruination, the Sundering, and a century of wandering the drowned ruins as caravan-tribes with nothing but numbers and stubbornness. Roughly 500 million people are alive worldwide, five percent of the ancient peak, and almost all of them live behind a Hearthstone's shimmer because the alternative is aberrants, wyrdstorms, and radiation.

Sorcerers found the Hearthbind Ritual in 75 TE and the first Basilica rose at New Tenochtitlan. Eighteen still function. One does not: Mount Hagen fell to the Hag-Queen Rangda in 130 TE while its peers watched, and behind its stolen barrier humans are livestock. Every Basilican diplomat knows the name and what it is for.

You are a citizen of Purgatory, the City-Isle in the Tides of Plenty, on an island that teleports. It began as the League's polite oubliette, where you sent the too-powerful-to-kill back when the Governor's Toll made residency a life sentence, and the founding stock of exiles, fugitives and inconvenient people never washed out of the character. The Waygates changed the island’s purpose about ten years ago: now every of the Basilica's goods, agents and secrets pass through the same crowded streets. The Council of Six governs day to day, the five Grand Factions hold five of its seats, and the Triune watch from a distance. Everyone came from somewhere else, everyone has an angle, and nothing is free — especially things that claim to be.

The recent history that is yours specifically
This matters more to a human character than any cosmology, because your character or their parents were in it.

153 TE — the Second Masquerade ends. The Shadow Directorate announced the Nightfolk to the League. In the monster hunts that followed, roughly 6,000 Nightfolk died and roughly four times that many humans. Humans did the hunting and humans took the worst of the casualties.
153–180 TE — the Ascendancy of Man. Twenty-seven years of pervasive anti-supernatural law. Not a distant era: it ended twenty years ago.
177 TE — the Awakened Censure Act, passed unanimously. Mages were blamed for the Sundering and for Mount Hagen, and taken to undisclosed prisons "for the public good." The Ascendancy laws were abolished in 180 TE. Nobody can tell you where the Awakened went, and Lhasa declines to dignify the question.
180 TE — the Pax Eldritus. Negotiated by a new generation, largely the scions of wealthy Sorcerer Clans. The Shadow Directorate became co-equal to the League Council. Do the arithmetic on your character sheet. Anyone over about 25 was alive under the Ascendancy. Anyone over 30 has childhood memories of it. Anyone over 47 remembers the year the world found out. Somebody in your family was on one side of that or the other, and in Purgatory it is considered very rude to ask which.

What Mundane Humans Know
The public face of the world, and all of it is true as far as it goes:

Nightfolk are legal. Register as a BRD  (Benign Reality Deviant) behave, and you hold the full rights of any citizen. Predation on humans is investigated by the Order of Vigiles; a confirmed breach revokes BRD status and issues a writ of execution to the Aberrant Hunters. Protection runs both ways: unsanctioned hunts are investigated too, and rogue hunters tend to go missing.
Discretion is still the convention. The law protects Nightfolk; it does not make anyone comfortable. Even the perception of using inhuman advantage over a human can cost BRD status, so most Nightfolk keep their nature to a trusted few. A human who works out what a neighbour is has been handed something dangerous to hold.
Sorcerers are respectable. Old clans, real academies at New Tenochtitlan, and the people who negotiated the Pax. The Awakened are the taboo, and asking about them is a way of becoming unpopular.
Paratech runs everything. Old World scitech goes haywire near a wyrdstorm; paratech is layered enchantment on etched leystone, standardized by the Glyphcode Standards, drawing on the Hearthstone field. Everyone knows a Greater Hearthstone runs on a subterranean quintessence reactor. Almost nobody knows how a reactor actually works.
Outside is lethal. Aberrants, anomalies, radiation, wyrdstorms. Two-thirds of the land is under water and most of the rest is uninhabited. And the things a citizen genuinely cannot know, which is where this splat's horror lives: what the Interstice was and why the survivors came out of it with a decade missing; who or what the Governor is; what happens in the Inner Reaches, which no expedition has crossed twice or mapped once; why heartglass grows toward the living; what the Fae-Hags of the Dreadmire mean when they call the Governor "It" and refuse to elaborate; how the Star-Chandlers know where the Isle will surface.

What to do with 21 freebies
You will not be spending 11 of them on an Art dot, so you have the largest effective creation budget in the game. Three purchases are worth more to a human than to anyone else:

Willpower. It is the mortal defence — you defend against Arts on permanent Willpower alone, or Grit alone against fear (Core Rules Section 8). It also pays for your Professional Practice and for Core Rules Section 8's mortal-resistance clause, out of the same nightly pool. Recommended: Willpower 5 at creation.

Psyche. Stability is Conscience + Self-Control, so every dot raises both your ceiling and your starting pool. With 7 dots and a minimum of 1 in Grit, the highest a human can start is Stability 6; freebies push toward 8, the top of core's stated range and higher than most Nightfolk will ever hold, since they are paying Eldritch off theirs.

Stamina. Vitality is Stamina + 7 and feeding damage lands at 1 lethal per 2 points (Core Rules Section 4). For a mortal, Stamina is a survival stat in a way it never was at the tabletop.

3. Where the Weight Actually Is
Humans are the only splat whose advantages are all structural. None is a dice bonus, and that is deliberate, a splat-wide bonus die is the one thing here that would force a core edit.

The whole sheet. Every XP goes to Attributes, Abilities, Merits and Backgrounds. A Nightfolk spending on Arts is not spending here, and Art dots also raise Resource, so they will keep spending there. Over a year the human simply has more of the sheet filled in (Core Rules Section 8).

Standing. A human working inside a human institution as a recognised participant has the circumstance advantage; staff should grant −1 difficulty on the relevant Social, Investigation, Law, Politics or Finance roll. This is Core Rules Section 10's circumstance lever, not a new bonus. In Purgatory the institutions that qualify are the licensed watch, the harbour and guild offices, the Order of Vigiles, the Promethean Institute, and the Sovereign Club. It does not apply in the Ashen Court or the Circle of Bone & Ivy, where a mundane human is tolerated at best, and it never applies to someone bluffing into a body they have no place in — that is Subterfuge.

The Pax is on your sheet. This is the human's real weapon and it costs nothing. Using an Art on an unconsenting human is precisely the predatory conduct that revokes BRD status and issues a writ; Core Rules Section 5 already charges the supernatural a Stability point for using an Art on a helpless mortal, and mortals always count as helpless. The Vigiles investigate. The Hunters execute. A Nightfolk who leans on a human in public has handed that human a live grenade with their name etched on it — which is why the smart ones do it where nobody is looking, and why a human who is seen is safer than a human who is alone.

Institutional access. Humans file complaints, testify, register grievances, pull records and appear before the Council without any of it being a problem. In a city where five factions all prefer their business unrecorded, the ability to make a thing official is closer to a weapon than it sounds.

Nothing is hunting you for what you are. No BRD registration, no writ risk, no Tether, no Attrition from ordinary play, no Backfire, no frenzy. The Core Rules Section 0 botch table's Art rows are dead letters on your sheet; when you botch you have simply failed badly, in public, like a person.

A high, clean Stability track. Eldritch 0, the highest realistic starting Stability in the game, no splat-specific bleed. Humans come apart more slowly than anything else in the setting.

Mortal resistance (Core Rules Section 8). Once per scene, spend 1 Willpower to become actively resisting against a single use of an Art — forcing a contest that would otherwise be uncontested under Core Rules Section 0 — or, in a contest already underway, to reroll a failed defence. Know this clause.

And the costs
Fully mortal. Soak is Stamina plus armour against bashing and lethal; aggravated is unsoakable (Core Rules Section 10). Healing runs the mortal table: a month at Crippled, two at Incapacitated. Nobody is spending Essence to close your wounds.

You are the pantry. A Stamina 3 human holds 10 Vitality; a predator who emptied you stayed latched into a fifth round and left you 5 lethal, two unmarked Health boxes, and a recovery rate of 2 a night while wounds that heal over weeks knit shut (Core Rules Section 4). Most humans never learn what happened. The law is on your side and the law arrives afterward.

You will lose most contests against Arts. Permanent Willpower alone, Grit alone against fear. A creation-standard human defends on 3 dice, 5 if you took the advice above. Say this plainly at application.

You are legible. In most Basilicas that means an address and a tax record. In New Colombia it means genealogy as devotional practice — the best place on Earth to trace a bloodline and the worst to hide one. In Purgatory it means the Vigiles have a file, the Ashen Court has a copy, and somebody sold them both.

The Pax is twenty years old. It replaced twenty-seven years of law that said your character's neighbours were vermin, and the generation that wrote those laws is not all dead. Humans are the majority whose parents did the hunting. That is a social vulnerability rather than a mechanical one, and it is the most useful thing in this document for a Storyteller who needs a scene.


4. Professional Practices
Each Profession grants one Practice: something the character can do because of who they are in this city, not because of anything supernatural.

The general rule. A Practice costs 1 Willpower and may be used once per scene. Unless it says otherwise, no roll is made; staff narrate the outcome. Practices are mundane. They are not Arts: they never roll Art + Resource, cannot be read by Intelligence + Eldritch, cannot be dispelled or contested as workings, do not botch, and never add dice to anything.

Why Willpower. It is the only currency a human has, it refreshes fully at midnight (Core Rules Section 3), and it is the same pool that pays for mortal resistance and for overriding an urge at low Stability. A Willpower 5 character has five points a cycle and three things to spend them on. That tension is the price, and core already issues it.

Practices leave traces. Each works by using a system, and in Purgatory systems are owned by factions. A used Practice is a fact somebody can buy.

6. Stability: Additional Triggers and Eldritch Pacing
Per Core Rules Section 5, this splat adds triggers for its own members and gives staff Eldritch guidance. It softens nothing. The core list applies in full, the roll is unchanged, and a trigger counts once per scene.

Additional triggers — mark one if your character, in this scene:

Denied what they saw. Talked themselves, or someone else, out of the truth in order to keep their life intact.
Used their standing against someone who had none. Turned a record, a credential, a complaint or a uniform on a person with no way to answer it. In Purgatory this includes the Pax itself: informing on a BRD you knew was innocent, or letting a writ proceed on evidence you knew was thin, marks here. The machinery that protects you kills people, and using it is a choice.
Broke the specific faith their Profession runs on. See each Profession. Where an added trigger and a core trigger describe the same act, mark one.

Recalibrating "witnessed something that should not exist"
This is the trigger that needs the most care in this setting, because in Purgatory the extraordinary is licensed. A registered Lilum drinking from a filed donor at the next table is not a trigger. A Garou changing shape in the street is a public-order matter, not an existential one. A Fae with a face like a lantern selling fruit on the Mangrove road is a Tuesday.

The trigger fires when the order fails to contain what you saw. Working examples:

The Mistcrown noticing you, or anything the Inner Reaches does with distance and time.
The bana'Shee's attention — the nightmares, the certainty of being watched.
Someone grown into the Ruby Coast reef, red and smiling and not finished dying.
A Grenveld village that just lost its two hundred and first resident, on schedule, to nothing.
Anything the Dreadmire coven says about the Governor while refusing to say what "It" is.
A Backfire Attention result (Core Rules Section 0), which is a self-administered trigger and awards no Eldritch for the sight itself.

Eldritch, for humans
Eldritch is the ledger of what a mind paid to see the world as it is. For a human it is pure cost: no Occult Arts for it to gate, so it buys exactly one thing — the Intelligence + Eldritch roll to read a working by sight — and charges a permanent Stability point that never returns.

Pacing guidance. Award Eldritch 1 only after repeated, undeniable contact the character chose not to look away from. One impossible night is a trigger, not a dot. A season on the thread is a dot. Award a second only for method: an Archivist reconstructing the Interstice from primary sources, a Physician cataloguing what heartglass does to tissue, a Ghostwright following a reactor conduit further than the schematic goes.

The specific human roads to Eldritch in this setting, all of them survivable and none of them free: the Interstice, the Inner Reaches, the quintessence reactors, heartglass and its will, the Governor, and where the Awakened went.

Two things to say to the player before the first dot lands. A human at Eldritch 2 with Conscience 3 and Self-Control 3 holds permanent Stability 4 and is one bad month from Slipping. And Eldritch is also a qualification — it lets a human trigger a prepared Working (Sorcerers Core Rules Section 7) and makes them a plausible candidate for register entry 2. The road out of being a Mundane Human runs through the thing destroying them.


7. Draining, Death, and the Two Exits
The Core Rules Section 9 item 6 checklist.

Health track. Standard seven levels, one track, wound penalties as written (Core Rules Section 10). No second track.

Soak. Stamina plus armour, difficulty 6, against bashing and lethal. Aggravated is unsoakable; no Merit here grants aggravated soak.

Healing. The mortal table in Core Rules Section 10 — this splat is that table's baseline. Aggravated heals at the lethal rate and nothing accelerates it.

Being fed upon. Feeding takes Vitality at 1 point per 1 Essence gained, no wound penalty from the loss, 1 lethal per 2 points taken — 1 per 3 if you are freely willing, which under the Pax usually means filed (Core Rules Section 4). The tally belongs to the victim and resets at midnight, so two predators in one night share one count. Vitality returns at midnight equal to your unmarked Health boxes: uninjured refills fast, battered barely at all, Incapacitated not at all.

Humans generate no Tether. The Utkinov Effect is a property of aspected essence. A human herd is safe to keep, which is exactly why humans are farmed rather than hunted, and why a filed donor arrangement is more secure for the Nightfolk than for the human.

At 0 Vitality. Pallid, freezing, barely lucid. Further feeding takes Health levels one for one as lethal, and that damage must begin to heal before Vitality refills (Core Rules Section 4).

At 0 Vitality, Attrition. Core Rules Section 3 names Mundanes in its Attrition list — a classical reality break — and Core Rules Section 4 says anyone drained to zero enters Attrition. Read together: a human at 0 Vitality rolls permanent Willpower at difficulty 6 at the end of each scene they remain there, loses control for the rest of the scene on a failure, and on a botch does not come back until the next midnight reset.

★ Flagged rather than ruled. Core Rules Section 3's Attrition condition is 0 Essence and humans have none, so the paragraph above is a reading of two core sections rather than a rule this splat may write. Core warns that "a splat stating a universal rule is a core gap wearing a splat's clothes," so it goes in Open Items instead of being asserted.

Past Incapacitated. Further lethal or aggravated damage kills. No corpus, no anchor, no vessel, no clause that turns it around. A dead human is dead, and in Purgatory a body is a commodity — the Vigiles look the other way where Amaia is concerned, the Bitterthorn does not return what it swallows, and the reef keeps what grows into it. Whether anything of a dead human persists is the Restless Dead document's business: not automatic, not owed, not a build plan.

Exit one: awakening (register entry 2)
A human may purchase Occult Arts as Nonaligned (becoming a Sorcerer) but only after undergoing the rite to become one via a ST scene. Vitality is consumed and replaced by an Essence pool. State the trade honestly: a Stamina 3 human trades 10 Vitality for 7 Essence, becomes a thinner meal, and gains something that can be taken — drained to 0 they lose their Arts and enter Attrition. They also acquire a registration question, a clan politics problem, and in Salamanca a capital one. The character leaves this document.

Exit two: ghouling (register entry 4)
A living human regularly fed Vitae becomes a Ghoul. Ghouling is a state, not a change of splat: the character stays a Mundane Human, keeps this document, and never holds Vitae as a pool. Whether Vitae does anything at all for a mortal Ghoul is deferred to the vampire document, and until it rules, the answer is nothing. What ghouling reliably provides is a domitor — and a Vigiles inquiry waiting to happen, since the line between a filed donor arrangement and a fed thrall is exactly the line the Pax was written to police.


The Six Professions
Each opens with the Profession's place in Purgatory, then its numbers. Every suggested build sums exactly to 7/5/3, 13/9/5, five Backgrounds and 21 freebies.


Enforcer
The watch, faction security, and the people who keep order under a twenty-year-old law

Enforcers keep order in the City-Isle — licensed watch, harbour security, faction muscle, private contractors, Vigiles auxiliaries, and the informal authority that keeps the crowded wards from going over. They are not Hunters. Aberrant Hunters are a splat of their own and their business is writs and the wilds; an Enforcer's business is this street, tonight.

An Enforcer knows Purgatory's rhythms: which wards are quiet, who the players are in a dispute, when to step in and when to let something burn itself out. In a city where five factions all have their own definition of order and the Council of Six has to be woken to arbitrate, that judgement is the job. The law is twenty years old. Nobody agrees on what the Pax means on the ground. The Enforcer decides, one call at a time, and lives with it.

They are also the humans most likely to be first at something that is not a registered anything — a body that was not killed by a person, a face nobody can hold in memory, a door in a wall that has no door. What they do next is the most character-defining question this Profession offers.

Practice — Known Quantity. Spend 1 Willpower, once per scene: the system works for you. Enter a sealed location, pull a Vigiles record or a harbour manifest, get a patrol to attend, no roll. It works because you are a recognised part of the apparatus, which means it works on the record.

Stigma — On the Roster. Your shift pattern, your address, your colleagues and your file exist, and in Purgatory a file is a tradeable good. Staff may always establish where you were and when. Any roll to conceal your identity or whereabouts from an institution suffers +1 difficulty.

Added trigger — Wore the authority for it. Mark if you used your standing to do something to a person you would have refused to do without it.

Faction fit. Order of Vigiles is the obvious home; the Sovereign Club pays better and asks worse; the Promethean Institute needs expedition security. An Enforcer with Ashen Court contacts is either compromised or very good at their job, and telling the difference is the campaign.


Broker
Merchants, fixers, and the people who know the price of everything

Purgatory is a freeport where every Basilica's goods, agents and secrets cross the same crowded streets, and the Broker is what makes that legible. They trade goods, information, favours and access, sometimes all four before lunch. The waygates opened about ten years ago and an older Broker remembers the before — when the Governor's Toll meant nothing left the Isle and being here was a sentence.

The stock in trade is specific to this city: restricted heartglass off the Ruby Coast, goblin fruit out of the Bitterthorn, raptor ichor rendering into unusually pure leystone, Old World salvage, League currency and Basilica futures, and the one commodity Purgatory produces natively, which is obligation. Favours here are recorded, sometimes literally. The Ashen Court supplies and the Sovereign Club launders, they loathe each other publicly and cannot function apart, and the Broker stands in the gap taking a percentage of both.

A Broker's asset is not inventory but reputation. Building it takes a career; losing it takes an afternoon. Most are not criminals in any meaningful sense — they are people who understand that rules are for those who cannot afford exceptions.

Physician
Doctors, surgeons, and the people who keep everyone alive

Physicians are indispensable anywhere, and in Purgatory they are something past it. The Isle produces injuries nothing in a textbook covers: ichor exposure from the refineries, goblin fruit in a patient who did not know what they were eating, wyrd-taint from too long outside the shimmer, and whatever the Ruby Coast does to tissue that lingers there. The pharmacopoeia is alchemical — Antananarivo reagents at Antananarivo prices — and half the clinical equipment is paratech that fails when the weather turns strange.

Post-Pax, your patients include BRDs, and their physiology is not in the textbook either. Some of them will not survive an honest chart. That is the whole of this Profession's moral problem: a wound recorded as inflicted by a Nightfolk starts a Vigiles inquiry, an inquiry can end in a writ of execution, and the Hunters do not investigate twice. What you write down decides who lives. Every faction knows it, and every faction has a Physician they maintain a quiet relationship with.

Archivist
Historians, scholars, and the keepers of what is known

Most pre-Sundering knowledge was destroyed. The people who preserve and transmit what remains are not clerks; they are performing an act of survival. Archivists are historians, scholars, librarians and researchers — the people who know where to find what is known, and increasingly what the gaps mean.

The gaps are enormous. The Interstice has no record at all: ten years in which evidence of human activity simply ceases, and survivors who came out the other side with a decade unaccounted for. The Itinerant century exists mostly in caravan oral accounts. And Purgatory has the worst record of any Basilica — it is barely eight decades old as a city, its founding population was exiles and fugitives, and its whole civic manner is that nobody's stated origin is challenged and nobody's stated origin is believed. Your archive is a monument built on false names.

What is here instead is volume: League correspondence passing through the freeport, faction ledgers, harbour manifests, Institute expedition reports, and the ruins on the Ghostlit Coast that match no known architecture and never have. Also the thing every Archivist eventually notices, which is that the record contains people who appear across generations without ageing, and that this used to be a scandal and is now simply BRD registration — except for the ones whose names predate any registry.

Shepherd
Community leaders, priests, and the voices the people follow

Every other Basilica has a cultural core. Purgatory does not, and that is the point — it has an exile creole instead of a heritage: elaborate courtesy over total mutual suspicion, obligation tracked like currency, and an allergy to questions about where anyone is from. Which means the Shepherd holds something the city itself lacks. A person who can make a genuine community out of dockside wards, Mangrove stilt-hamlets and Grenvalden-descended families has built the one thing no faction can purchase and no Art can counterfeit.

Shepherds here come from everywhere: Salamanca-trained clergy on a long posting, ward representatives who have been fighting the Council since before the waygates, spirit-mediators trained on the Kathmandu model, and people who arrived and gradually became who everyone went to. Some sell threshold wards on the Mangrove road — and an honest Shepherd knows which of their stock is genuinely functional, which came from an Artificer, and which is comfort, because a mundane human works no magic and pretending otherwise gets parishioners killed.

There is also the Grenveld, where no settlement may hold more than two hundred and the two hundred and first drops dead, and where much of the City-Isle's native human stock comes from. Every Grenvalden family has done the arithmetic on a birth. If you shepherd those people, you have been asked questions no doctrine answers.

Ghostwright
Glyphcode mechanics, salvagers, and the people who make dead technology work

Paratech runs the world, and the Glyphcode Standards mean a device etched in Bogotá can be repaired in Chino. A Ghostwright is who repairs it. They are the mechanics, salvagers, hackers and improvisers who keep the grid alive, bring Old World scitech back from the dead, and jury-rig from leystone offcuts things the guilds would price at a year's wages.

Know the boundary, because it defines the Profession: a Ghostwright works no magic. Novel enchantment is the trade of Artificers and sorcerers. What Glyphcode literacy buys is everything else — reading a formula, repairing it, rerouting it, bypassing it, cannibalising one device into another, and diagnosing why a conduit that passes inspection is quietly wrong. In a world where the qualified practitioners are few, expensive, and mostly employed, that is not a lesser skill. It is the reason the lights stay on in the wards nobody bills for.

It also runs straight into the illegal trades. Registration-evasion work — a paratech piece that hides an inhuman feature from a Vigiles inspection — is the most in-demand and most dangerous commodity a Ghostwright can make, because the Pax is built on being able to see what someone is. Add Glyphcode forgery, unlicensed grid taps, salvage that was never declared, and red leystone, which pound for pound holds ten times what purple does and wants to merge with living things, so nobody sane builds with it and somebody always does.

The parties who notice are the Promethean Institute, which owns discovery and licensing and is Cordial with everyone; the Order of Vigiles, whose remit is paracausal crime; and the Sovereign Club, which funds the Institute and dislikes competition. Their attention starts friendly. That is the frightening part.

A Note on Playing Mundane Humans
Nightfolk are more powerful in direct terms — more durable, more capable in specific domains, less exposed. Your Willpower 5 defence will lose to a capstone Art most nights it is tested.

What humans have is different in kind. Roots, in a city that has none. The whole XP budget pointed at a single sheet, which after a year means you are simply better at being a person than anyone else in the room. A Stability track with nothing eating it. Access to the institutions that actually run the City-Isle, used openly, by someone with a legitimate place in them.

And the Pax. Read it as a human, not as a supernatural: it exists because 6,000 Nightfolk died in a single panic and four times that many humans died causing it, and it holds because both sides remember. Nightfolk need it more than you do. They are registered; you are not. They can lose their status; you cannot. A Physician forty years into practice knows things no Lilum does. An Archivist a decade into the manifests knows this city from the inside. A Shepherd with their ward's real trust can do something no Art replicates.

The question is not how to compete with the Nightfolk. It is what ordinary people do with persistence, community, and a life lived in the open, in a city where nothing is free — especially the things that claim to be.

Humanity survived the end of the world through sheer persistence. You are the proof that it worked.
